# FOSIT geography data

Default geography tables for FOSIT ([METHODOLOGY.md §3.1](../../../docs/METHODOLOGY.md#31-geography)).
Every file here is generated by [`core/tools/build_geo_data.py`](../../tools/build_geo_data.py)
from US Census Bureau / OMB files. Those are works of the US Government and are in the
public domain (17 U.S.C. §105). Do not edit the CSVs by hand: change the builder and rebuild.

## Files

| File | One row per | Columns |
|------|-------------|---------|
| `zcta_to_geo.csv` | 2020 ZCTA in the 50 states + DC (33,642 rows) | `zcta5, county_fips, state, cbsa_code, geo_id, land_share` |
| `cbsa_attributes.csv` | CBSA in scope (387 metro + 538 micro) plus one `{ST}-NONCBSA` unit per state that has counties outside any CBSA (44) | `geo_id, geo_name, state, population, treatment_eligible, kind` |
| `state_attributes.csv` | State (50 + DC) | same columns as above, `kind = state` |
| `SOURCES.json` | - | URL, vintage, retrieval date, size and SHA-256 of every source; row counts and SHA-256 of every output; build checks |

All CSVs are UTF-8, comma-separated, with one header row and no comment lines.
The attribute files follow [`schema/geo_attributes.table.json`](../../../schema/geo_attributes.table.json)
plus an extra `kind` column (`metro`, `micro`, `noncbsa` or `state`).

Column notes:

- `county_fips`: 5-digit county FIPS of the county the ZCTA is assigned to. Connecticut uses
  planning-region codes (09110-09190), matching the delineation and population files.
- `state`: USPS code. In `zcta_to_geo.csv` it is the state of the assigned county.
  In `cbsa_attributes.csv` it is the **first state named in the CBSA title**, e.g.
  `New York-Newark-Jersey City, NY-NJ` → `NY`. OMB titles name the state of the first principal
  city first (e.g. `Washington-Arlington-Alexandria, DC-VA-MD-WV` → `DC`), so in practice this is
  the principal city's state. It was not cross-checked against the principal-cities file.
- `cbsa_code`: empty when the county is outside every CBSA.
- `geo_id`: the CBSA code, or `{ST}-NONCBSA` (e.g. `MT-NONCBSA`) for ZCTAs outside any CBSA.
- `land_share`: share of the ZCTA's land area inside the assigned county, 4 decimals.
- `population`: Census Vintage 2025 resident population estimate for 1 July 2025, summed
  over counties. A multi-state CBSA's population includes its counties in every state.
  A `{ST}-NONCBSA` unit holds the population of that state's counties that are in no CBSA.
  CBSA + NONCBSA populations add up exactly to the sum of state populations.
- `treatment_eligible`: `true` for CBSAs and states, `false` for `{ST}-NONCBSA` units
  (eligible as controls only, see §3.1).

## Assignment rule

1. Each ZCTA is split into ZCTA-county parts by the 2020 Census relationship file.
2. The ZCTA goes to the county with the **largest land area** part. Ties are broken by larger
   water area, then by lower county FIPS.
3. The county maps to a CBSA through the July 2023 OMB delineation (list 1). A county that is
   in no CBSA maps to `{ST}-NONCBSA` for its state.

## Connecticut

In 2022 Connecticut replaced its 8 legacy counties (09001-09015) with 9 planning regions
(09110-09190) as county equivalents. The 2023 delineation and the population estimates
use planning regions. The 2020 ZCTA-county file uses the legacy counties.

The builder fixes this with the Census 2022 county-subdivision-to-ZCTA relationship file for
Connecticut (`acs22_cousub22_zcta520_st09.txt`). The first 5 digits of a 2022 county
subdivision GEOID are its planning region. For every ZCTA that touches Connecticut, the
legacy-county parts are dropped and replaced by the cousub parts summed to planning region.
Parts in neighbouring states (MA, NY, RI) are kept from the 2020 file. The largest-land-area
rule then runs as everywhere else.

The two files use different boundary vintages (2022 vs 2020), so a ZCTA's CT land area differs
by at most 1.2% between them (13 of 288 ZCTAs differ by more than 0.1%). `land_share` uses
the sum of the ZCTA's parts as its denominator, so it never exceeds 1. Example: `06103`
(Hartford) → Capitol Planning Region `09110` → CBSA `25540` Hartford-West Hartford-East Hartford.

## Scope: Puerto Rico and territories excluded

Only the 50 states and DC are included. 149 Puerto Rico ZCTAs and the 10 Puerto Rico CBSAs
are dropped (listed in `SOURCES.json` under `checks`). The Island Areas are not in the source
files. Orders shipped to these ZIPs show up as unmapped.

## Known limitations

- **ZIP is not ZCTA.** ZCTAs are Census approximations of USPS ZIP Code delivery areas, built
  from 2020 Census blocks. Most residential ZIPs have a ZCTA with the same code, but USPS ZIPs
  change over time and the match is not exact.
- **PO-box-only and single-site ZIPs are unmapped.** ZIPs with no residential area (PO boxes,
  large organisations, military) have no ZCTA. FOSIT counts and reports such ZIPs; it never
  drops them silently. Users who need them can supply their own `zip → geo_id` mapping.
- **Land area, not population, decides a split ZCTA.** A ZCTA that crosses a county line goes
  wholly to the county holding most of its land, even if most of its people live in the other
  county. `land_share` shows how clean each assignment is; about 30% of ZCTAs have
  `land_share < 1`.
- **Vintages differ.** Geography is 2020 ZCTAs, 2023 CBSAs and 2025 population estimates.
  A county's population is current; the ZCTA boundaries are not.
- **Principal-city state.** `state` for a multi-state CBSA is taken from the title (see above),
  not from a separate principal-cities file.

## Rebuilding

Download the four sources into a directory outside the repo (URLs are also in `SOURCES.json`):

```sh
SRC=/tmp/fosit_geo_src && mkdir -p "$SRC" && cd "$SRC"
curl -fO https://www2.census.gov/geo/docs/maps-data/data/rel2020/zcta520/tab20_zcta520_county20_natl.txt
curl -fO https://www2.census.gov/geo/docs/maps-data/data/rel2022/acs22_cousub22_zcta520_st09.txt
curl -fO https://www2.census.gov/programs-surveys/metro-micro/geographies/reference-files/2023/delineation-files/list1_2023.xlsx
curl -fO https://www2.census.gov/programs-surveys/popest/datasets/2020-2025/counties/totals/co-est2025-alldata.csv
```

Then, from the repository root (Python 3.10+, standard library only):

```sh
python core/tools/build_geo_data.py --src "$SRC" --retrieved YYYY-MM-DD
python -m unittest tests.test_geo_data -v
```

`--retrieved` is the download date written into `SOURCES.json`. Compare the `sha256` values in
`SOURCES.json` to check that you downloaded the same files. Identical inputs and the same
`--retrieved` date give byte-identical outputs.
